# Lens Critique — EDUCATIONAL / TEACHING-AID DESIGN

**Game:** 《一起去买菜》 (consumption lesson 消费课， from a money-values course for left-behind children aged 6–12, taught by a welfare organisation in primary schools)
**Frameworks applied:** CPA / concreteness fading (Bruner 1966; Singapore MOE 2012; Fyfe et al. 2014), worked examples & backward fading / expertise reversal (Sweller; Renkl & Atkinson 2003; Kalyuga et al. 2003), backwards design / constructive alignment (Wiggins & McTighe; Biggs & Tang), Montessori prepared environment (Lillard 2005; Barrett et al. 2015).
**Scope respected:** flow, station order, 14 rules, engine and `cards.json` content untouched by every fix below — all fixes are presentation, prompt copy, sequencing *within* a station, or feedback rendering.

**The learner this is judged against:** a child whose money is normally counted, held and spent by grandparents or other caregivers. She has likely never held 12 coins and decided alone. She may be 6 (pre-fluent reader, counting to 20) or 12 (bored by anything babyish) in the same classroom, with one teacher for ~40 children — so the game itself, not an adult, must do the modelling, bridging and prompting.

---

## Finding 1 — The budgeting relationship is never made pictorial at the moment of decision (CPA break)

**(a) Problem + evidence.** Money representation is dual-coded at the *display* level (drawn coin row + numeral in `Coins.tsx`, price gems on cards), but the lesson's core structure — *my coins = recipe gap + bus fare still owed + what's actually free to spend* — exists only as one abstract text line buried in the wallet drawer: `walletHint: '菜谱还差 {a} 币 · 车费 {b} 币 · 还能自由花 {c} 币'` (`src/i18n/zh.ts:86`, rendered only in `WalletDrawer.tsx`). The data is already computed (`selectors.walletSummary`: `missingCost`, `busDebt`, `freeEstimate`) but a child must voluntarily open the wallet to see it. At the actual decision point — a card selected in the market — the only number shown is a bare remainder, `买完还剩 {n} 币` (`MarketPanel.tsx:234`). Screenshot `r10a_market_selected_zh_desktop.png` shows the consequence: the child is about to buy 豆腐 (not in her 红烧鱼 recipe) and the screen tells her only "还剩 7 币" — nothing connects those 7 coins to the 姜/蒜 she still needs or the bus fare still coming. There is no bar-model-style pictorial bridge anywhere: coins are never shown *partitioned* into "for the recipe / for the bus / free", so the child meets budgeting purely as abstract numerals.

**(b) Why it matters for this audience.** CPA's central finding is that novices cannot start at the abstract stage: the numeral "7" carries no structure for a child who has never budgeted real money — and this audience, uniquely, has almost *no* concrete money experience to anchor numerals to (caregivers handle money for them). Fyfe et al.'s concreteness-fading result says the fix is not more concreteness everywhere, but an explicit pictorial bridge that is present while the concept is new. The Singapore bar model exists precisely for this: part-whole relationships (a whole split into named parts) are the pictorial form of budgeting. Right now the game jumps Concrete (coin pictures) → Abstract (arithmetic remainders) and skips the Pictorial thinking tool entirely — at exactly the station （练 · 觉察消费冲动） where the part-whole judgement "can I afford this *and still finish my recipe*" is the whole lesson.

**(c) Concrete fix (presentation only).** Add a **coin bar** wherever a purchase is being decided (market + store selection area, and inside the wallet drawer above the existing text line): a horizontal strip of coin cells equal to the child's current coins, segmented and colour/label-coded into three parts — 菜谱还差 (recipe gap, from `missingCost`), 车费 (bus debt/fare, from `busDebt`), 自由花 (the rest, from `freeEstimate`). When a card is selected, the cells its price would consume light up and animate out of the *free* segment first; if the price would eat into the recipe segment, those cells pulse — no words, no error, the bar itself shows "this bite comes out of your recipe money". All inputs already exist in `walletSummary`; zero engine/data change. The existing text line stays as the abstract caption under the bar (Concrete→Pictorial→Abstract on one screen).

**(d) Severity: High.** This is the consumption lesson's central concept, currently untaught at the point of use.

## Finding 2 — No worked example precedes the child's first real decisions; the bots model actions but never think aloud

**(a) Problem + evidence.** The only models available to a novice are the three bots, and their narration is purely factual. Every log template reports *what* happened, never *why*: `'{name}买了{item}'`, `'{name}把{item}放进先等等夹'` (`src/i18n/zh.ts:296–317`). The steady bot buying a recipe ingredient and the impulsive bot buying a sticker are narrated in identical form — a watching child cannot tell a good decision from a poor one, and the single most valuable move in the game, using 先等等 to delay an impulse, is never verbalised by anyone ("我好想要贴纸…先放进先等等，买完菜再说" appears nowhere). The child's first consequential decision (market, lap 1) may come before she has seen even one complete bot turn, since the starting player rotates per station. The one fully-worked example the project owns — the model parent dialogue in `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md` §06 （小禾： "差鸡蛋，要 3 个，我只有 1 个") — exists only in the spec document, not in the game.

**(b) Why it matters for this audience.** Worked-example research (Sweller; Renkl) is unambiguous for novices: studying an *annotated* example beats observing outcomes or solving cold, because the annotation carries the reasoning schema. In this course's real setting — one teacher, a room of children, grandparents who cannot coach money talk at home — there is no adult available to supply the missing narration. If the game doesn't think aloud, nobody does. Un-annotated bot behaviour is worse than neutral for the follower/impulsive patterns: children imitate salient actions (Zip buying the sticker is fun and visible), and imitation without reasoning imports the *behaviour* without the *judgement*.

**(c) Concrete fix (copy/presentation only).** (i) Add short **reason bubbles** for bots at decision moments, keyed to the existing log events at the presentation layer: steady bot on a recipe buy — "先买菜谱里的菜"; steady bot passing a temptation — "贴纸先等等，菜还没买齐"; impulsive bot using the hold clip — "好想要…先放先等等吧"; impulsive bot at the parent station — the scripted help-seeking already implied by `rfl-imp-2`. These are new strings beside existing log templates, rendered as speech bubbles (the `SpeechBubble` component already exists), not engine changes. (ii) On the child's **first** market visit only (lap 1), add one coach line under the stall fan that models the target behaviour against the actual stall contents, e.g. "先找菜谱里的菜：你还缺姜和蒜" — composed from `missingIngredients`, which selectors already expose. (iii) Surface the REQUIREMENTS model dialogue at the parent station — see Finding 3.

**(d) Severity: High.** Modelling is the main teaching channel available to this audience, and it is currently silent.

## Finding 3 — Parent station （练 · 表达需求与反思）: no model answer, and feedback confirms without teaching — the thinnest scaffolding sits on the audience's hardest pain point

**(a) Problem + evidence.** This station is the game's direct answer to pain point 3 (these children struggle to express their own needs), yet in the scripted/offline mode — the mode guaranteed to run in classrooms, since live mode needs an API key — a successful answer produces *no content feedback at all*: the engine simply advances `step + 1` (`src/game/stations/parent.ts`), the UI adds a `✓ 说清楚了` chip (`ParentPanel.tsx:602`), and the only verbal feedback in the whole exchange is the closing line "OK, that is clear. Here are {n} coins." The parent never echoes what the child said ("你想买鸡蛋，还差 2 个币 — 说清楚了"), so the child never hears her own need spoken back in a complete, well-formed sentence — the very artefact she is supposed to take away. Before the dialogue, the entry screen asks her to commit （现在用 / 这次先不用， screenshot `r10b_parent_fan_zh_desktop.png`) having shown only the scoring rule in small print; no example of a good answer is ever shown, although a complete model dialogue sits unused in `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md` §06. (The live-LLM mode does respond warmly to content — its pacing prompt mandates it — which makes the offline mode, the dependable one, the pedagogically weaker one.)

**(b) Why it matters for this audience.** For a child whose presenting difficulty *is* formulating a need, an unmodelled cold question ("你想要几个币？") reproduces the exact real-life situation in which she already fails — standing in front of a busy adult, put on the spot. Learning science for expressive skills is consistent: model first (worked example), then supported production, and feedback must *name the successful move* to consolidate it ("you said what AND how many — that's why it worked"). A checkmark confirms; it does not teach. There is also a dignity dimension the design itself recognises elsewhere (compliant plans must be approved, no "why did you waste money"): a child who answers unclearly twice currently gets the same fallback line and a silent mode-switch to picture cards — kind, but she never learns what was missing from her sentence.

**(c) Concrete fix (copy/presentation only).** (i) **Model before production:** when the station opens (first use), show the spec's model dialogue as two soft, visually-distinct "示范" bubbles — parent asks Q1, a child answers fully ("我还差鸡蛋，要 3 个币") — captioned "像这样说，就可以". Skippable, shown once. (ii) **Echo feedback in scripted mode:** after each cleared answer, render a parent bubble that mirrors the child's answer content before the next question — composable at the panel layer from the chosen option's label or the matched keyword ("想买鸡蛋 ✓ 说清楚了") — no engine change, mirroring how the R9 status indicator was added presentation-side. (iii) **Sentence-starter chips** above the free-text input: "我想买…", "我还差…", "下次我先…" — tappable starters that insert text, directly lowering the formulation barrier for typing children. (iv) On an unclear answer, vary the fallback to name the missing piece ("还差一点：告诉我**几个币**就好") — the question's target is known per step, so the panel can pick the matching hint string.

**(d) Severity: High.** This is the flagship station for the project's stated mission; its scaffolding should be the strongest in the game, and is currently the weakest.

## Finding 4 — The reflection does not consolidate: generic options are decoupled from the child's own game

**(a) Problem + evidence.** The closing reflection (`src/screens/Reflection.tsx`, `cards.json` `reflections.humanOptions`) offers six fixed statements — "I bought all my recipe food", "I skipped the sticker", "I traded for food I needed", … — from which the child picks one, regardless of what she actually did. A child who bought two stickers can still select "I skipped the sticker"; the statement is *chosen*, not *recognised from her own history*. Bot reflection lines are likewise personality scripts (`rfl-imp-1` "I got the sticker and had fun…") that may not match what that bot did this game. Meanwhile the game already possesses the child's true history: the event log, and the Feast ledger （看看钱花到哪了） with grouped subtotals （菜/诱惑/车费/意外）. The ledger itself is optional (behind a button on the Feast screen) and the reflection screen makes no reference to it — the sequence Feast → ledger → reflection *could* form a consolidation chain, but the links are missing.

**(b) Why it matters for this audience.** In backwards-design terms, the reflection is part of the lesson's Stage-2 evidence: it is where the teacher (and the child) find out whether the enduring understanding — "I decide where my money goes" — landed. Retrieval practice only consolidates when what is retrieved is the learner's *own* experience (self-reference effect); picking a plausible-sounding card from a menu is a recognition task about a generic child, not a reflection on *my* game. For children with little adult-guided reflection at home, this screen may be the only structured "think about what you did" moment in the whole lesson — spending it on fiction wastes the course's scarcest resource. It also quietly teaches that reflection means "say the approved sentence", the opposite of the parent station's honest-expression goal.

**(c) Concrete fix (presentation only).** (i) **Personal recap line** above the options, composed from state the UI already derives: "这一局的你：买齐了菜 ✓ · 用过 1 次先等等 · 找家长帮了 1 次忙 · 还剩 2 币" (from `state.log` / ledger derivation already implemented in `Feast.tsx`). (ii) **Match-and-highlight:** pre-highlight the one or two preset options that are *true* of this child's game (recipe completed → rho-1 glows; a temptation was held then dropped → rho-2; a swap happened → rho-3) and sort true options first — all options remain selectable, free-text stays; only ordering, highlight and the recap are new. (iii) Make the Feast ledger the default-expanded first view rather than a button detour (flow order unchanged — it is already on the path to reflection).

**(d) Severity: Medium-High.** The consolidation moment of the lesson currently certifies statements that may be false.

## Finding 5 — Scaffolding never fades across three laps, and one presentation serves ages 6 and 12 alike

**(a) Problem + evidence.** Laps 1, 2 and 3 present identical support: the same instruction lines ("翻开 6 张卡…点一张，再选怎么做"), the same help-card tip, the same after-buy remainder, the same reflection prompt. Nothing is withdrawn as the child gains competence, and nothing extra is offered to a struggling younger child. There is no lap- or performance-contingent presentation anywhere in the panels (support copy is static strings). The developmental span is six years — the widest in primary schooling — yet the game has a single register: what scaffolds a 6-year-old in lap 1 is, by lap 3, redundant load for a 10-year-old who has already completed two recipes.

**(b) Why it matters for this audience.** This is the expertise-reversal effect (Kalyuga et al. 2003) in its classic form: scaffolding that accelerates novices becomes extraneous cognitive clutter once schemas form, and — just as important motivationally for 10–12-year-olds — it reads as *babyish*, which is a real attrition risk for the older half of the course's age band in a classroom where peers can see each other's screens. Renkl & Atkinson's answer is a fading *schedule*, and this game has a natural one built into its fixed flow: three laps are three passes over the same task class (4C/ID's task classes with decreasing support). The flow cannot change — but the support level per lap is pure presentation.

**(c) Concrete fix (presentation only, keyed off `state.lap`).** Lap 1 = full support (coach lines, reason bubbles, goal tags, model dialogue). Lap 2 = reduced: instruction lines collapse to icons + the 求助牌 carries the detail; bot reason bubbles stop except for the hold-clip move. Lap 3 = minimal: no instruction line in the market/store panels, reflection prompt upgrades to a forward-looking stem for children who completed their recipe ("下次去买菜，你会先做哪一步？") while keeping the standard stem otherwise. All conditional rendering/copy in existing panels; no config or engine change. (If even lap-keyed copy is judged too much, the minimal viable version is lap-3 instruction-line removal only.)

**(d) Severity: Medium.** Not a blocker for a first course run, but the difference between a demo and a teaching aid over a 6-year age span.

## Finding 6 — Prepared environment: the goal disappears behind the modal while deciding, and the fan hides card names

**(a) Problem + evidence.** Two environment failures against the Montessori test — *can the child see her goal, her materials and her next action without asking an adult?* (i) While the market modal is open, the recipe checklist lives in the dimmed bottom bar *behind* the popup (screenshot `r10a_market_selected_zh_desktop.png`: the basket strip is half-covered by the modal), so at the exact moment of choosing, "what am I still missing" is the least visible thing on screen. (ii) In the fan, cards overlap so heavily that names are physically truncated — in the same screenshot 胡萝卜 is clipped and two adjacent 姜 cards are indistinguishable except by position; R10_DESIGN §6 already flags this as open risk 1. A pre-fluent 6-year-old reader cannot reconstruct a clipped name and must guess from a cropped picture. (iii) Each station *has* a learning-goal tag (`练 · 觉察消费冲动` etc., defined in `src/i18n/cardText.ts:200–207`), but the station panels show only the station name — the intention is invisible to the learner (and to a circulating teacher glancing at screens).

**(b) Why it matters for this audience.** Prepared-environment research (Lillard 2005; Fisher et al. 2014; Barrett et al. 2015) shows that what the environment makes visible, children use; what it hides, they must hold in working memory or ask about. This audience has the least adult availability to ask. Working-memory load is also the binding constraint for the youngest children: remembering a 4-item recipe, a price, a remainder and a rule simultaneously, while the recipe itself is visually occluded, exceeds a 6-year-old's capacity — the predictable behaviour is not "wrong learning" but random tapping, which the no-fail design then silently rewards. An environment that keeps the goal in view converts memory load into a perception task, which is precisely the Montessori move.

**(c) Concrete fix (presentation only).** (i) Pin a **mini recipe-progress strip inside every station panel header** (ingredient icons with ✓ / still-missing states, from `missingIngredients` — the same strip the bottom bar shows, repeated where the eyes already are). (ii) Apply R10_DESIGN §6's already-designed fallback for the fan: on selection/inspect, the card's full name floats large above the fan; additionally, never overlap two *identical* cards adjacently without a count badge ("姜 ×2"). (iii) Show the existing `练 · …` goal as a small chip under the panel title — the strings exist; this is a one-line render. All three are layout/copy in existing components.

**(d) Severity: Medium.** Directly determines whether the youngest third of the audience can play *deliberately* rather than by tapping.

## Finding 7 — Event-station choices carry no consequence feedback, so "leave room for uncertainty" is asserted, not experienced

**(a) Problem + evidence.** The event station's stated goal is 练 · 为不确定留余地. Its two option cards are presented symmetrically, but unlike the market there is no consequence preview on the spend card (no "花完还剩 n 币" equivalent), and after choosing, the log records the choice factually (`eventChoice: '{name}：{item}'`) with no line connecting it to the lesson — e.g. after paying 1 coin for a warm drink in `evt-thirsty`, nothing notes the trade the child just made or that her remaining coins still cover the recipe; after choosing the free option, nothing notes that she *kept* a coin for later. The structure even mis-teaches in places: on good events (`evt-extra-egg`, `evt-neighbor-greens`) the free option yields the same ingredient as the spend option, so the only learnable rule from outcomes alone is "never spend" — the intended judgement (spending on others/comfort can be a legitimate choice) is invisible because outcomes never comment on it.

**(b) Why it matters for this audience.** Feedback that teaches (as opposed to confirms) must make the *consequence structure* visible at or immediately after the choice — this is the feedback analogue of the CPA bridge: the abstract principle "keep a buffer" needs a pictorial/concrete instance ("you kept 2 coins — that's your bus fare, safe") attached to the child's own decision, in the moment. These children get almost no such narrated money feedback in daily life; the game is the one place it can be systematic. The events are also the only *unpredictable* spending in the game, i.e. the only rehearsal for real-life financial surprises — the course's core promise.

**(c) Concrete fix (copy/presentation only).** (i) Put the market's remainder preview on the spend option card: "花 1 币 · 还剩 {n} 币" (price and coin count are already in scope in `EventPanel`). (ii) After a choice, show one consequence-echo line in the panel before it closes, composed from existing state: if she spent and the recipe is still covered — "花了 1 币，买菜的钱还够"; if she spent into the recipe gap — "注意：买菜还差 {n} 币了"; if she chose free — "1 个币也没花，留给后面的车费和买菜". Three string templates with `{n}` fills, rendered panel-side from selectors; no engine or data change.

**(d) Severity: Low-Medium.** Small surface, but it is the only place the lesson's "buffer" concept can be *felt* rather than recited.

## Already good — keep and build on these

- **Dual-coded money everywhere.** Coins are always drawn (a countable row of coin discs) *and* numbered (`Coins.tsx`; R10's `CoinCounter` keeps the pictorial row) — the pictorial representation is present at the representation level; Finding 1 is about extending it to the *relationship* level, not fixing an absence.
- **A textbook CPA descent on failure at the parent station.** When a typed answer isn't understood, the game doesn't repeat the question louder — it drops one representation level, from abstract text to picture cards, with a gentle line ("That's OK. Let's pick a picture together."). That is exactly "return to the earlier stage when stuck", implemented with dignity.
- **先等等 (hold clip) is a genuine concrete manipulative.** Physically setting a desired card aside, carrying it, and re-deciding later at the same price *embodies* delayed gratification enactively rather than lecturing about it — the strongest single learning design in the game, and well matched to 练 · 觉察消费冲动.
- **The wallet hint has the right budgeting schema.** "菜谱还差 a · 车费 b · 还能自由花 c" is precisely the part-whole decomposition a budgeting bar model would draw (Finding 1 is about its placement, not its content).
- **The Feast ledger is a real consolidation artefact.** Spending grouped into 菜 / 诱惑 / 车费 / 意外 with subtotals and a leftover line is abstract-stage reflection material of good quality — it just needs to be wired into the reflection (Finding 4).
- **Psychological safety of the environment.** No ranking, no elimination, no negative money; the bus debt is a paperclip （待付夹）, auto-repaid, with copy that insists it is not a punishment; an incomplete dish still goes on the table. In prepared-environment terms: error is information, never shame — essential for children with low expressive confidence.
- **The 求助牌 is Montessori independence done right.** Help is self-served, always available, free of penalty, and *personalised* — `helpHints` derives missing items, coins, debt and a gentle suggestion from the child's actual state rather than showing generic instructions.
- **Parent-station ground rules protect the learner.** Compliant plans must be approved; no interrogation about "wasting" money; picture cards are a full equivalent of typing, not a lesser track; one question at a time. The station's *rules* are exemplary — Findings 3 is about its feedback and modelling catching up with its rules.
- **Per-station learning intentions exist and are honest.** Every station carries a `练 · …` goal that names a transferable money behaviour (not a content fact) — clean UbD Stage-1 work. They only need to be visible (Finding 6) and evidenced (Finding 4).
- **Lap-end BasketReview gives social information without comparison** — others' progress is shown as "3/4", never ranked — modelling the classroom norm the course wants, and quietly supplying the peer information the follower-personality lesson depends on.
